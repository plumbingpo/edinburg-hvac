How El Paso Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Many mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

How old is your system? That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.
